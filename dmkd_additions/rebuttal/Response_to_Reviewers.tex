\textbf{Action Editor (AE.1):} \textit{``Reviewer 2 notes that variance is rarely reported and that significance of differences is not computed. Please report variability across runs and, where you claim improvements over baselines, provide a test that supports the claim.''}

\noindent\textbf{Response:}\\
\noindent\textbf{Difficulty Estimation Experiments (Section 6.1).} We conducted a complete statistical evaluation of the difficulty estimation experiments across all three French benchmarks: \texttt{Sentences Books} ($N = 960$ test sentences across 6 CEFR levels A1 to C2), \texttt{LjL} ($N = 413$ youth literature texts across 4 ordered levels), and \texttt{Sentences Internet} ($N = 480$ unanimously annotated web sentences across 6 CEFR levels). Using non-parametric bootstrap resampling with $B = 1{,}000$ iterations with replacement over each test set, we compute the sample mean and bootstrap standard error ($\mu \pm \sigma$) alongside 95\% confidence intervals for five complementary metrics: classification Accuracy, Macro-F1, Mean Absolute Error (MAE, in integer CEFR steps), Quadratic Weighted Kappa (QWK), and the normalized Pairwise Mismatch rate ($PM_{\text{norm}}$, expressed as a percentage of all $\binom{N}{2}$ pairs).

To test whether explicit CEFR system prompts improve prediction accuracy over unprompted models, we run two-sided paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests on instance-level ordinal prediction errors $|y_i - \hat{y}_i|$ across all datasets, alongside a Holm--Bonferroni correction across comparisons (Table~\ref{tab:rebuttal_table2}, Table 3 of the manuscript). Historical baseline models ($N = 16$) and normalized Pairwise Mismatch rates are reported in Table 2, and bootstrap $\text{QWK} \pm \sigma$ scores of historical confusion matrices are reported on Figure 8. As observed by Reviewer 2, not every numerical difference is statistically significant. The results show that adding the CEFR system prompt yields statistically significant gains on \texttt{Sentences Books} for both models and on \texttt{Sentences Internet} for GPT-4o. In contrast, the prompt effect is not statistically significant on \texttt{LjL} for either model, nor for GPT-4o-mini on \texttt{Sentences Internet}, even after Holm--Bonferroni adjustment. We state these non-significant outcomes explicitly in Section 6.1.2.

\noindent\textbf{Recommender System Experiments (Section 6.3).} For the content-based and collaborative filtering recommendation experiments in Section 6.3, the results of the submitted version came from a single training run per model. We re-trained every model with its optimal hyperparameters (Table 12 of the revised manuscript) over 5 random seeds, without any new hyperparameter search, so that these runs measure the robustness of the selected configurations. Seed~0 is the original run: it reproduces exactly the published figures of ALS, LightGCN and KNN, whereas BPR and LMF are not deterministic even with a fixed seed, because the Implicit library trains them with multithreaded stochastic gradient descent. KNN is deterministic and evaluated once. Table~\ref{tab:rebuttal_rs_results} (Table 10 of the revised manuscript) reports the mean and standard deviation of each metric over the seeds. To compare two models on a dataset, we average the NDCG@5 of each test user over the seeds and apply a two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test to the paired per-user scores, with a Holm--Bonferroni correction over the 20 comparisons of Table~\ref{tab:rebuttal_rs_tests} (Table 11 of the revised manuscript).

LightGCN initialised with LLM embeddings remains the best model on the four datasets (ADA on Zeeguu, ml-100k, and Goodreads; BERT on Tomplay). However, as Reviewer~2 suspected, not every difference reported in the submitted version is significant. As detailed in Table~\ref{tab:rebuttal_rs_tests}, the ADA initialisation significantly improves over Xavier on Zeeguu, Goodreads, and Tomplay, but not on ml-100k. The BERT initialisation significantly improves over Xavier on Goodreads and Tomplay, but not on Zeeguu or ml-100k. These results on BERT must be read together with the truncation of its inputs to 128 tokens (Table~\ref{tab:rebuttal_token_lengths}, Response \textbf{R1.3}): BERT brings no improvement on Zeeguu, where it encodes only 14.5\% of the text of the articles, whereas on Tomplay, the only dataset where BERT receives the full text of every item, it significantly improves over Xavier and outperforms ADA. This pattern is consistent with truncation explaining part of the gap between ADA and BERT, although a single untruncated dataset does not allow us to separate this effect from differences between encoders and between datasets. % Checked 27/10 against the 5-seed 6.3 discussion, 6.3.4 and abstract.
We therefore corrected the sentences of Section 6.3 that described the improvement as uniform across datasets and embeddings, replacing single-run gains by the multi-seed means. We note two limitations of this analysis: hyperparameters were selected with seed~0, which slightly favours this seed for less stable configurations (for example, LightGCN with BERT on Goodreads, standard deviation $0.0058$), and the paired per-user test does not account for variability between seeds, so that on large catalogs (Goodreads and Tomplay) small relative differences reach very small $p$-values.
\end{mmblock}

\begin{reviewbox}
\textbf{Action Editor (AE.2):} \textit{``The empirical justification of the weight $w_1$ (p.\ 14) rests on a single dataset and a single LLM; please broaden this or state the limitation explicitly.''}
\end{reviewbox}

\noindent\textbf{Response:}
We expanded both the theoretical derivation and the multi-model empirical validation of $w_1$ across all 9 simplification systems and 1,000 test sentence pairs (500 from \texttt{Sentences Books} and 500 from \texttt{Sentences Internet}). Analytically, $w_1^* = 0.8055$ compensates for the anisotropy of contextualized CamemBERT embeddings: unrelated French sentences exhibit a high cosine similarity floor $S_0 = y_2 \approx 0.810$, whereas identical sentences yield $y_1 = 1.000$, compressing the effective semantic similarity range to $\Delta S = y_1 - y_2 = 0.190$. Over the same boundary regimes, the proxy simplification accuracy shift spans $\Delta A = x_2 - x_1 \approx 0.046$. Equalizing the marginal contributions yields:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rebuttal_w1_derivation}
\begin{aligned}
w_1^* &= \frac{\Delta S}{\Delta S + \Delta A} = \frac{y_1 - y_2}{(x_2 - x_1) + (y_1 - y_2)} = \frac{0.190}{0.190 + 0.046} = 0.8055 \approx 0.80, \\
w_2^* &= 1 - w_1^* = 0.1945 \approx 0.20.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
To verify that model comparisons do not depend on a narrow choice of $w_1$, we swept $w_1 \in [0.05, 0.95]$ across all 9 fine-tuned and zero-shot LLMs on the 1,000 test pairs. As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:rebuttal_sensitivity}, the relative ranking of all models remains strictly invariant across the operational interval $w_1 \in [0.15, 0.85]$ (Spearman rank correlation $\rho = 1.0000$, zero rank crossovers). Figure~\ref{fig:rebuttal_pareto} complements this sensitivity sweep by mapping the Pareto frontier between Simplification Accuracy ($A$) and Semantic Similarity ($S$), showing that fine-tuned models dominate zero-shot baselines across the $(A, S)$ plane.

Let $C \in \mathbb{N}^{K \times K}$ be the confusion matrix where $C_{u,u'} = \sum_{i=1}^N \mathbb{I}(y_i = u \land \hat{y}_i = u')$ counts instances with true level $u$ and predicted level $u'$ (including exact matches $C_{u,u}$ on the main diagonal, with $\sum_{u,u'} C_{u,u'} = N$). Grouping instance pairs $(i, j)$ by their confusion matrix cells $(u, u')$ and $(v, v')$, the number of pairs formed between the two cells is the product $C_{u,u'} \cdot C_{v,v'}$, which explains the multiplications in Figure 2. Normalizing by $\binom{N}{2} = \frac{N(N-1)}{2}$ with weight $1$ for strict inversions ($v < u, v' > u'$) and $\frac{1}{2}$ for ties yields $PM_{\text{norm}} \in [0, 1]$:
\begin{equation}
PM_{\text{norm}} = \frac{1}{\binom{N}{2}} \sum_{u=1}^{K} \sum_{u'=1}^{K} C_{u,u'} \left( \sum_{v=1}^{u-1} \sum_{v'=u'+1}^{K} C_{v,v'} + \frac{1}{2} \sum_{v=1}^{u-1} C_{v,u'} + \frac{1}{2} \sum_{v'=u'+1}^{K} C_{u,v'} \right).
\label{eq:rebuttal_pm_norm}
\end{equation}
Diagonal cells $C_{u,u}$ (exact matches) are explicitly included in the outer sum: an exact match at $(u, u)$ still forms an inverted pair with any instance in $(v, v')$ where $v < u$ and $v' > u$. Algorithm~\ref{alg:rebuttal_pm} gives the revised pseudocode.

\begin{algorithm}[!htbp]
\caption{Normalized Pairwise Mismatch ($PM_{\text{norm}}$) Computation via Confusion Matrix Counts}
\label{alg:rebuttal_pm}
\footnotesize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require True ordinal labels $y = (y_1, \dots, y_N) \in \{1, \dots, K\}^N$, predicted ordinal labels $\hat{y} = (\hat{y}_1, \dots, \hat{y}_N) \in \{1, \dots, K\}^N$
\Ensure Normalized Pairwise Mismatch score $PM_{\text{norm}} \in [0, 1]$
\State Initialize confusion matrix counts $C_{u,u'} \gets 0$ for all $u, u' \in \{1, \dots, K\}$ \Comment{Includes diagonal exact matches $C_{u,u}$}
\For{$i = 1$ to $N$} $C_{y_i, \hat{y}_i} \gets C_{y_i, \hat{y}_i} + 1$ \EndFor
\State $M_{\text{total}} \gets 0$ \Comment{Accumulator for weighted pairwise violations}
\For{$u = 1$ to $K$}
    \For{$u' = 1$ to $K$}
        \If{$C_{u,u'} > 0$}
            \State $S_{\text{strict}} \gets \sum_{v=1}^{u-1} \sum_{v'=u'+1}^{K} C_{v,v'}$; \quad $S_{\text{tie}} \gets \frac{1}{2} \sum_{v=1}^{u-1} C_{v,u'} + \frac{1}{2} \sum_{v'=u'+1}^{K} C_{u,v'}$
            \State $M_{\text{total}} \gets M_{\text{total}} + C_{u,u'} \times \left( S_{\text{strict}} + S_{\text{tie}} \right)$
        \EndIf
    \EndFor
\EndFor
\State $P_{\text{total}} \gets \frac{N(N - 1)}{2}$; \quad $PM_{\text{norm}} \gets \frac{M_{\text{total}}}{P_{\text{total}}}$; \quad \Return $PM_{\text{norm}}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
